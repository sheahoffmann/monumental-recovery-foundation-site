Our story

We built this for the man who is ready and can't pay for it.

Monumental Recovery Foundation started with a problem we kept running into: the treatment that gives a man the best chance at a lasting recovery is the treatment almost no one can afford.

The window is short.

Anyone who has sat with a family in the middle of this knows the shape of it. There is a window — sometimes hours, sometimes a few days — when a man is willing. Willing to stop, willing to leave, willing to let somebody help him. Everything in his life has been pushing against that willingness for years, and it will start pushing again soon.

What happens inside that window usually comes down to money. Not to readiness, not to how much his mother loves him, not to whether he would do the work if given the room to do it. Money.

Insurance covers a stretch of days. The family scrapes together what it can. The program that would actually give him a fighting chance costs more than what is on the table, so he goes somewhere cheaper, or shorter, or nowhere. And the window closes.

Time is the ingredient nobody wants to pay for.

Extended care is not a luxury version of treatment. It is the version that accounts for how people actually change. The first weeks are detox, stabilization, and a nervous system learning it is safe. The work that rearranges a life — honesty, accountability, repairing relationships, building a routine a man can carry home — happens after that, and it takes months.

Cut the timeline and you keep the expensive part and lose the part that lasts. Families feel this. They watch someone come home after a short stay, still raw, still without a foundation, and they brace for the next call.

So when we set out to fund something, we did not want to fund a few more days. We wanted to fund the whole runway.

Why a foundation, and why men.

We are a small organization by design, focused on one thing we understand well: getting adult men into extended, high-quality treatment and paying for it.

Men are who we know how to reach. They are also, in our experience, the ones most likely to wave off help, minimize what is happening, and wait until the situation is dire. When one of them finally raises his hand, we want the answer to be yes, and we want it to be yes today — not after a fundraiser, not after a waiting list.

A foundation lets us hold charitable dollars that are ready before the phone rings, and to put them where they do the most good: against the actual invoice for the actual bed.

What we believe

Four convictions we are not flexible about.

A man's bank balance is not a prognosis.

Financial need is a reason to help, not a reason to expect less. We award on eligibility and need, case by case, without regard to race, color, national origin, religion, disability, or any other protected status.

Quality is not negotiable.

We only fund programs that meet standards our Board has set and reviews: proper licensure or certification, evidence-based practice, and a clean health, safety, and ethics record.

Dignity includes privacy.

No one has to become a fundraising story to get help. We protect applicant and recipient information and get written consent before sharing anything identifiable.

Recovery is a beginning, not a finish line.

The point is not a completed program. It is a man with a life worth protecting — work, relationships repaired, a reason to stay.

Honesty about who we are

Our founders work in this field. Here is how we keep that clean.

The people who started this foundation come out of the treatment world. That is why we know where the gaps are — and it is also a conflict of interest we take seriously rather than quietly.

Our bylaws require every director, officer, and committee member to disclose any ownership, employment, or governance role in any organization that provides substance use treatment, when they join and every year after. Anyone with an interest in a program under consideration is recused: not counted toward quorum, not part of the discussion, not part of the vote.

Approving an affiliated program for our list, or sending a scholarship to one, takes an affirmative vote of the directors who have no interest in it. Before that vote, those directors have to document that the program meets the same standards as every other approved program and that its rates are at or below what comparable unaffiliated programs charge. We review affiliated-program awards annually and report them as required.

Applicants may tell us where they would prefer to go, and we take that seriously. The final decision rests with the committee, based on eligibility and the best clinical fit.

Where we are now

Founding stage, and building in public.

Monumental Recovery Foundation is newly organized as a Colorado nonprofit corporation. Our Board is seated, our bylaws and conflict-of-interest policy are adopted, and our application for 501(c)(3) recognition is in process.

Right now we are doing three things: building the fund that pays for scholarships, finalizing written eligibility criteria and an application process, and assembling the list of approved programs we will send men to.

If you want to help — as a donor, as a treatment provider who meets our standards, or as an advisor with clinical or recovery expertise — this is the useful moment to reach out.
